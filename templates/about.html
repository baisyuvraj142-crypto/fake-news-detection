About the Project & System Architecture

Comprehensive overview of NLP preprocessing, TF-IDF feature extraction, and ML classification.

How the System Works (In Simple Terms)

When a user submits a news headline or article, the system converts human-written English text into numerical patterns that machine learning models can understand. It follows a multi-stage pipeline:

1. Cleaning & Filtering
Converts text to lowercase, removes punctuation, symbols, numbers, and hyperlinks to eliminate noise.
2. Stopwords & Lemmatization
Removes frequent non-informative words ("the", "is", "at") and reduces words to their grammatical root form ("running" → "run").
3. TF-IDF Vectorization
Computes unigram & bigram weights. Rare words carry higher distinctiveness while common terms are scaled down.
4. Classification & Top Words
The trained model outputs REAL or FAKE with a probability score and highlights the top 5 words influencing the decision.
Data-Leakage Prevention (FR-5)

In many public news datasets (e.g. ISOT from Kaggle), real articles often contain giveaway prefixes like "WASHINGTON (Reuters) -", which allows a naive model to score ~99% purely by memorizing the word "Reuters" rather than learning genuine linguistic patterns of fake news. Our preprocessing explicitly strips these publisher signatures, and completely ignores subject and date metadata columns to ensure genuine linguistic learning.

Evaluated Algorithms

  • 1. Logistic Regression: A linear model that applies the sigmoid function to map feature weights to a probability between 0 and 1. Coefficients provide full transparency into which specific words pushed the prediction towards REAL or FAKE.
  • 2. Multinomial Naive Bayes: A probabilistic classifier based on Bayes' Theorem with independence assumptions between features. Extremely fast and well-suited for high-dimensional word counts.
  • 3. Passive Aggressive Classifier: An online margin-based algorithm that updates its weights aggressively upon misclassification and remains passive when a correct prediction is made with sufficient margin.

System Limitations (Section 10)

  • Style vs. Facts: The model learns writing style, tone, and vocabulary patterns. It does not query external live databases or fact-check real-time political claims.
  • Domain and Temporal Scope: Trained predominantly on modern English political and general news. Historical news, niche scientific papers, or non-English articles may have lower accuracy.
  • Satire and Irony: Satirical articles (e.g. from The Onion) or strong editorial opinion pieces may occasionally be misclassified due to stylistic nuances.
  • Input Length: Requires at least 20 words (FR-22) because very short headlines do not provide sufficient TF-IDF feature density.

Viva Quick-Reference Guide

File / Component Role in Project
src/preprocess.py Shared text cleaning, regex cleaning, lowercase, stopwords removal, and NLTK WordNet lemmatization.
src/train.py Loads dataset, prevents data leakage, fits TF-IDF, trains 3 models, selects the best F1 model, and saves artifacts.
src/predict.py Loads model once at startup, validates minimum 20 words, computes confidence and top 5 explainability words.
app.py Flask backend serving Web UI routes and developer REST API (/api/predict).
tests/test_basic.py Automated test suite checking cleaning, validation, REST API, and acceptance criteria.
